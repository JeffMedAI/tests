## 1. BUYER PERSONA ANALYSIS

### Understanding the GP Surgery Buying Ecosystem

GP surgeries are small businesses with NHS contracts. They are not typical enterprise buyers — there is no procurement team, no IT department, and no innovation budget with a ringfenced mandate. Buying decisions are made by a small number of individuals who are simultaneously managing a clinical service under pressure. Every interaction must respect their time and speak directly to their operational reality.

---

### Persona 1 — The Practice Manager (PRIMARY BUYER)

**Who they are:** Practice Managers run the day-to-day operations of a GP surgery — staff rotas, supplier contracts, CQC compliance, patient complaints, IT systems, premises, and every administrative function. They often do this with minimal support staff and no technical background. In smaller practices, the Practice Manager is also the receptionist supervisor, HR lead, and finance controller. There are approximately 7,500 GP practices in England; almost all have a Practice Manager.

**What they care about:**
- Reducing call volume pressure on reception staff (this is their single biggest daily pain)
- Not introducing anything that creates clinical risk or patient harm
- CQC compliance — anything that touches patient workflow must be documentable and safe
- Value for money — they are spending practice funds, not NHS central budget
- Simplicity of onboarding — they cannot commit weeks of staff time to implementation
- Proof it works before they sign anything

**What they fear:**
- Patient complaints arising from AI interaction
- GDPR breach (even perceived risk is disqualifying)
- A tool that creates more admin work than it saves
- Being "sold to" — they have seen enough healthcare SaaS pitches that promise and underdeliver
- Being the first — they are not early adopters

**What wins them over:**
- A peer reference: "Churchtown have been using it for 3 months and it works"
- Specific time savings stated in minutes per call, calls per day
- Simple pricing (flat monthly, no per-call surprises)
- On-premise architecture (data stays in the building — this eliminates their biggest GDPR fear immediately)
- A live demo they can show the GP partners in 10 minutes

**Where to reach them:**
- LinkedIn (many are active; search "Practice Manager NHS" or "Practice Manager GP")
- NHS Networks / Local Primary Care Networks (PCN) meetings — physical and online
- LMC (Local Medical Committee) newsletters and events — LMCs often share supplier news with member practices
- Practice Management Network (PMN) — UK's largest practice manager community
- Direct email outreach (NHS practice email addresses are publicly listed on NHS.uk)
- GP trade press (Pulse, GPOnline) — they read these for operational guidance

**Agent role in targeting this persona:**
- Research and build a list of Practice Manager names + emails from NHS.uk surgery finder (public data, no purchase needed)
- Draft personalised cold email sequences
- Monitor LinkedIn for Practice Manager posts about call volume/reception pressure — these are warm leads
- Track responses in CRM and advance pipeline stage

---

### Persona 2 — The GP Partner (FINANCIAL APPROVER)

**Who they are:** GP Partners own a share of the practice as a business. They hold financial accountability alongside their clinical role. In most practices, any spend above a threshold (typically £200–500/month) needs GP Partner sign-off. They are time-poor and largely delegate operational decisions to the Practice Manager — but they can block a purchase.

**What they care about:**
- Practice financial health — cost vs. return
- Patient safety and CQC standing
- Not being exposed to reputational or regulatory risk
- Staff retention (if reception staff are less stressed, that matters)

**What wins them over:**
- ROI framing: "X minutes per call × Y calls per day = Z hours of staff time returned"
- Data safety proof: on-premise architecture, no cloud, GDPR-clean
- A colleague's endorsement ("Dr [name] at Churchtown says it's been worth it")
- Short commitment path — monthly contract, cancel anytime, low switching cost

**Where to reach them:**
- Medical press: Pulse, GPOnline, BJGP (British Journal of General Practice)
- RCGP Annual Conference (29–30 October 2026, SEC Glasgow) — ideal for brand building
- BMA channels and LMC newsletters
- LinkedIn — GP Partners who post about practice management are findable

**Agent role:** Draft GP Partner-specific email variant (emphasise ROI and safety, not feature detail). Research GP Partners at target practices via NHS.uk and LinkedIn.

---

### Persona 3 — The PCN Clinical Director / Network Manager (PHASE 2–3 BUYER)

**Who they are:** Primary Care Networks (PCNs) group ~30–50 GP practices into a network. The PCN Clinical Director (usually a GP partner with a network leadership role) and the PCN Manager/Network Manager oversee shared initiatives. They commission services at network level and can introduce a product to 30+ practices simultaneously. There are approximately 1,300 PCNs in England.

**What they care about:**
- Network-level efficiency gains
- Demonstrating innovation to their ICB (Integrated Care Board)
- Cost per practice (they need to justify this across the network)
- Standardised implementation — something that works at scale, not just in one surgery
- NHS data governance compliance

**What wins them over:**
- Multi-practice case study (unavailable until Phase 2 — but Churchtown is the seed)
- Clear cost-per-practice figure
- Implementation model that doesn't require them to manage it practice-by-practice
- ICB endorsement or pilot funding route

**Where to reach them:**
- PCN Development events (PCN Plus Live, similar)
- NHS England PCN forums and toolkits
- ICS/ICB working groups focused on primary care capacity
- LinkedIn — search "PCN Clinical Director" or "Primary Care Network Manager"

**Agent role:** Phase 2 priority. Build PCN contact list. Draft a PCN-specific one-pager once Churchtown case study is available. Research which PCNs contain Churchtown and identify the Clinical Director.

---

### Persona 4 — The ICB Digital Lead (PHASE 3 BUYER / COMMISSIONER)

**Who they are:** Each of England's 42 Integrated Care Boards (ICBs) has a Chief Digital Information Officer (CDIO) or digital/transformation lead. They can commission products at population level — across all practices in their geography.

**What they care about:**
- Alignment with NHS long-term plan (reducing primary care burden)
- NHS procurement compliance (frameworks, DSIC, Data Security and Protection Toolkit)
- Evidence base — clinical effectiveness, safety record, patient outcomes
- Integration with EMIS/SystmOne (the two dominant GP clinical systems)
- Value for money at population scale

**What wins them over:**
- Published evidence (case study, ideally peer-reviewed)
- G-Cloud or DSIC framework listing (makes procurement straightforward)
- Data Security and Protection Toolkit (DSPT) completion
- EMIS/SystmOne integration roadmap
- Clear pricing model for multi-practice volume

**Agent role:** Phase 3. Begin DSPT completion research. Research G-Cloud 15 / DSIC application timeline. Draft ICB commissioner briefing pack (on trigger from DOCUMENT_REGISTRY.md).

## 2. CHANNEL STRATEGY

### Overview

JeffLocal is pre-revenue, founder-led, with no sales team and a minimal budget. Every channel must be either free or near-free, and must produce work that an AI agent can execute autonomously (drafting, researching, sending, tracking). Channels that require significant human time to run continuously are deprioritised until Phase 2 revenue justifies them.

The channel mix is ordered by: (1) cost to start, (2) fit with buyer personas, (3) agent-executability.

---

### Channel 1 — Direct Outreach to Practice Managers (Email)

**Why this channel:** Practice Manager emails are publicly listed on NHS.uk (every GP surgery must publish its contact information). This is a legally accessible, free lead source requiring no data purchase. Direct email is the highest-converting B2B channel for healthcare when the message is relevant and the sender is credible.

**Target audience:** Practice Managers at GP surgeries in North West England (starting with Southport/Lancashire geography, expanding nationally in Phase 2)

**Specific tactics:**
- Build a list of GP surgeries in target geography using NHS.uk Find a GP tool (public, no scraping needed — manual research or lightweight scripting)
- Find practice email addresses and, where listed, named Practice Manager
- Send a three-email sequence over 3 weeks (see Section 8 for draft copy)
- Email 1: Cold introduction — what JeffLocal does, data safety, pilot reference (once permitted)
- Email 2: Follow-up — specific time-saving framing, CTA to see a 10-minute demo
- Email 3: Final — low-pressure close ("happy to answer questions, no obligation")
- Track opens, replies, demo bookings in CRM

**Agent role:**
- Research and compile lead list from NHS.uk (public data)
- Personalise emails with practice name, location, approximate list size (from NHS data)
- Send sequences using email automation tool (see Section 6)
- Log all responses in CRM, advance pipeline stage
- Flag warm responses (replies, link clicks) for Saeed's personal follow-up

**Success metric:** Demo booking rate ≥ 5% of sequences sent (industry benchmark for cold healthcare outreach is 2–4%; personalised on-premise safety message should beat this)

**Phase:** Phase 1 (build list, draft sequences, queue for Saeed approval) → Phase 2 (send)

**Constraints:** All email copy requires Saeed approval before sending. Do not send until Churchtown pilot is stable and Saeed has confirmed it is ready to reference.

### Channel 3 — NHS / ICB Procurement Routes

**Why this channel:** NHS organisations prefer to buy from assured frameworks — it reduces their procurement overhead and risk. Getting on a framework is a one-time investment that opens a national buying route. This is not a short-term channel but it is strategically essential for Phase 3 scale.

**Route A — G-Cloud (Crown Commercial Service)**
G-Cloud is the UK government's cloud services marketplace. Any public sector body (including NHS) can buy from it without running a full tender. G-Cloud 15 is the current iteration (launched March 2026, valid 18 months). JeffLocal's on-premise architecture is unusual for G-Cloud (which is cloud-focused) — however, the "Software" lot could accommodate the dashboard/reporting component.

- **Action:** Research G-Cloud 15 supplier application requirements. Draft a G-Cloud service definition for JeffLocal dashboard (SaaS-like component). Apply when Data Security and Protection Toolkit (DSPT) is complete.
- **Timeline:** Phase 2 preparation (Q3 2026)
- **Agent role:** Research application requirements, draft service definition, flag DSPT requirements to Saeed

**Route B — DSIC (Digital Services for Integrated Care) / NHS Buying Catalogue**
The NHS Buying Catalogue (via DSIC framework) is the primary route for GP clinical software. Being listed makes procurement straightforward for any ICB or practice. Requires Baseline Assurance Standard (BAS) completion and DSPT. Contact: england.dsic@nhs.net

- **Action:** Begin DSPT self-assessment. Research BAS requirements. Draft supplier application when product is production-hardened.
- **Timeline:** Phase 2 (Q4 2026)
- **Agent role:** Research BAS requirements, draft DSPT self-assessment framework, prepare application dossier for Saeed review

**Route C — NHS Shared Business Services (SBS) Healthcare AI Solutions Framework**
A new £900m framework launched May 2026 (NHS SBS) specifically for AI solutions across NHS organisations. Runs 2027–2035. This is the most directly relevant long-term procurement vehicle for JeffLocal.

- **Action:** Monitor NHS SBS for supplier application window. Prepare submission when framework opens.
- **Timeline:** Phase 3 (2027)
- **Agent role:** Set a research reminder for Q4 2026. Draft framework submission document when window opens.

**Route D — ICB Direct Pilot Commissioning**
Many ICBs have innovation funds and can commission local pilots outside formal frameworks. The path is: identify the ICB Digital Lead, share an innovation brief, propose a funded pilot.

- **Action:** Identify which ICB covers Churchtown (Cheshire & Merseyside ICB). Research their digital innovation team. Draft an ICB Commissioner Briefing Pack (document in DOCUMENT_REGISTRY.md future items).
- **Timeline:** Phase 2 (Q3 2026, after Churchtown case study approved)
- **Agent role:** Research ICB structure, identify named contacts, draft commissioner briefing pack

**Success metric:** At least one framework listing or ICB pilot proposal submitted by end of Phase 2

## 6. PLUGIN, SKILL & MCP RECOMMENDATIONS

For each tool below: what it does, why it helps this specific pipeline, and whether it is likely available on the Claude MCP registry.

---

### 6.1 Email Outreach — Instantly.ai or Lemlist

**What it does:** Sends personalised cold email sequences at scale, tracks opens/clicks/replies, auto-follows up, integrates with CRM.

**Why it helps:** Agent can draft sequences, system sends them automatically. Removes manual email-sending. Tracks engagement signals that feed pipeline stage advancement.

**Cost:** Instantly.ai free tier limited; Lemlist from ~£39/month. Phase 2 justified by first pilot revenue.

**Agent executable?** Yes — agent drafts copy, uploads to tool, schedules send. Saeed approves sequences before they go live. Replies come to Saeed's inbox — agent monitors and flags.

**MCP registry:** Not confirmed as native MCP. However, both have REST APIs — a custom tool call or n8n workflow could integrate. **Search MCP registry for "email outreach" or "Lemlist" to check.**

**Phase 1 alternative:** Use Gmail directly via Claude in Chrome MCP for small-scale send (up to 50 contacts manually). Scales to dedicated tool at Phase 2.

---

### 6.2 LinkedIn Research & Outreach — PhantomBuster or LinkedIn Sales Navigator

**What it does:** PhantomBuster automates LinkedIn profile research, list building, and connection request sending. Sales Navigator is LinkedIn's native B2B search and outreach tool.

**Why it helps:** Agent can research Practice Manager profiles, compile lead lists, and draft connection messages. PhantomBuster can send connection requests on Saeed's behalf (with his approval on message copy).

**Cost:** PhantomBuster free trial then ~£56/month. Sales Navigator ~£99/month.

**Risk:** LinkedIn's terms of service prohibit automated outreach at scale. PhantomBuster operates in a grey area — use at low volume (under 20 connections/week) to stay safe.

**Phase 1 alternative:** Manual LinkedIn research by agent (Claude in Chrome MCP can read LinkedIn pages); Saeed sends connections manually.

**MCP registry:** Search for "LinkedIn" or "PhantomBuster". LinkedIn does not offer an official public API for outreach — any MCP would be unofficial.

---

### 6.3 CRM — HubSpot MCP

**What it does:** Allows Claude to read and write HubSpot CRM contacts, deals, and pipeline stages directly via tool calls.

**Why it helps:** Agent can update CRM without Saeed having to log in. After each email sequence reply, agent updates the pipeline stage. Weekly CRM reports are auto-generated.

**MCP registry:** HubSpot has a published MCP server. **Search MCP registry for "HubSpot" — this is likely available.**

**Phase:** Install at Phase 1 alongside CRM setup.

---

### 6.4 Calendar & Scheduling — Calendly

**What it does:** Provides a booking link so prospects can self-schedule a demo call. Integrates with Google Calendar and sends automatic reminders.

**Why it helps:** Removes the back-and-forth of scheduling. Saeed's email outreach can include a Calendly link — interested prospects self-select and book. Agent can monitor Calendly for new bookings and trigger pre-demo briefing pack send.

**Cost:** Free for basic single event type.

**MCP registry:** Calendly has an API. **Search MCP registry for "Calendly" — may be available.**

---

### 6.5 Web Research — Perplexity / Tavily / SerpAPI

**What it does:** Agent-accessible web search tools that allow Strategy Agent to research GP practice details, NHS procurement updates, competitor activity, and press coverage automatically.

**Why it helps:** Agent can monitor for competitor news, search for new leads, check NHS.uk for practice data, and track press coverage — all without Saeed involvement.

**MCP registry:** Tavily and Perplexity both have published MCP servers. The current Claude setup already has WebSearch capability. **Confirm via ToolSearch whether Tavily or Perplexity MCP is installed.**

---

### 6.6 Document Creation — DOCX / PPTX Skills (already installed)

**What it does:** Creates polished Word and PowerPoint files from agent-generated content.

**Why it helps:** Agent can produce the one-pager PDF, demo slide deck, press release, case study, and pilot proposal as properly formatted documents ready for Saeed's review.

**Status:** Already available as skills (docx, pptx, pdf). Use these for all deliverable documents.

---

### 6.7 NHS Data Research — NHS.uk API / Find a GP

**What it does:** NHS.uk publishes structured data about every GP surgery in England (name, address, contact details, practice type, patient list size). Available via the NHS ODS (Organisation Data Service) API — free, public, no key required.

**Why it helps:** Agent can programmatically build a complete list of all GP surgeries in a target geography, including practice email addresses. This is the primary lead source.

**How to use:** NHS ODS API endpoint: `https://api.service.nhs.uk/organisation-lookup-api/search` — accepts postcode radius or ICB geography as parameters. Returns structured JSON.

**Agent executable?** Yes — Strategy Agent can write a Python script (via Bash tool) to query NHS ODS API and output a structured lead spreadsheet. No authentication required for public data.

**MCP registry:** No specific NHS MCP exists, but the API is callable from Bash. Script should be in `scripts/marketing/` directory.

**Phase 1 action:** Write NHS ODS data pull script for North West England GP surgeries. Output to CSV. Store in `docs/marketing/leads/`.

---

### 6.8 Email Monitoring — Gmail MCP

**What it does:** Allows agent to read Saeed's Gmail inbox (with permission), identify replies to outreach sequences, and flag warm responses.

**Why it helps:** Agent can monitor for prospect replies overnight and surface them in the morning report, meaning Saeed sees warm leads immediately without having to scan his inbox.

**MCP registry:** Google Workspace / Gmail MCP exists (via Google's official MCP server or third-party). **Search MCP registry for "Gmail" or "Google Workspace".**

**Constraints:** This requires Saeed to grant Gmail access to the MCP. Handle with care — this gives agent access to Saeed's personal inbox. Scope to marketing-related contacts only if possible.
