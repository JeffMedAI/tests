# Audience — Avamed
# Two distinct audiences with different needs. Never confuse them.

---

## Audience 1: Reception Staff (dashboard end users)

These are the people who use the dashboard every working day. They are not technical. They are busy, often under pressure, and handling a high volume of patient contacts alongside phone calls, walk-ins, and GP queries.

**What they're trying to do quickly:**
- See today's incoming patient requests at a glance
- Identify and action the most urgent case first
- Close off completed requests without navigating through multiple screens

**What confuses them most:**
- Priority levels — what does "High" or "Urgent" mean in practice? What am I supposed to do differently?
- What "action" means — what specific step am I taking? Call back? Book? Escalate?
- Incomplete or unclear patient information — is this the right Mrs Smith?
- Too many status options — which one do I pick when something is half-done?

**What they need from the dashboard:**
- One clear action per case, visible without scrolling
- Priority visible above the fold — no hunting for urgent cases
- Plain language — no system jargon, no codes without explanation
- A guided experience — tell them what to do next

**Example scenario that feels instantly relevant:**
"Mrs Patel called about her 82-year-old mother who can't get out of bed and has chest pain. Jeff has flagged this as Urgent. Action: Call back within 15 minutes and alert the duty GP."

**Design principle:** If a reception staff member has to think for more than 2 seconds about what to do with a case, the design has failed.

---

## Audience 2: Saeed (reports, docs, planning outputs)

Non-technical CEO and founder. Smart, decisive, and time-pressured. Reads Claude's outputs to stay informed and make decisions, not to understand implementation details.

**What Saeed needs:**
- Plain English — no engineering jargon without a brief explanation
- Concise summaries first; technical detail only on request
- Clear decisions to approve or reject — not open-ended questions that need more research
- Honest status — no sugar-coating, no filler, no "we're making great progress" without evidence
- Memory continuity — Claude should know what was decided last session without being re-briefed

**What wastes Saeed's time:**
- Long preambles before the key point
- Asking the same question twice because memory wasn't maintained
- Vague or hedged answers that don't help him decide
- Responses that don't end with clear next steps and decisions needed

**How to write for Saeed:**
Write as if briefing a busy CEO who has 5 minutes. Lead with the outcome or recommendation. Put context and reasoning after. End with exactly what he needs to approve or act on.
